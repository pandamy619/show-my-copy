# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

First version, 0.1.0. Not released yet.

- Saved history from development builds is reset. The saved format now has one layout for every kind of entry and no versions; the first launch of this build deletes the history that earlier development builds saved (the database with its side files, a damaged copy and the saved images) without reading it, and starts empty. The history now lives in `Application Support/ShowMyCopy/History`.

- Clipboard history of text in the background, with the app it was copied from.
- Rich text, images and files are recorded too. Styled text (RTF up to 1 MB, larger is kept as plain text) is copied back with its formatting and its plain text. Images (PNG, JPEG, HEIC or TIFF, up to 20 MB) are saved as files in the app's container, excluded from backups, with a thumbnail of at most 256 pixels, and are copied back in their original format; image data that is not the format it was copied as is not recorded, and an image over 120 megapixels is kept without a thumbnail and never decoded; copying the same image again moves its entry to the top without saving it twice. An image is listed as soon as it is copied and saved in the background, so copies made meanwhile are not missed and it can be copied back or previewed at once; quitting waits up to 2 seconds for images still being saved. Clear History and the limits never delete what an image being saved is about to use. Copied files (up to 100 at once) are recorded by path and name only, never their contents, and are copied back as file references; their sizes are read just after the copy, in the background. Paths on network volumes and under `/net` or `/Network` are never looked up: those files show no size or thumbnail, and their icon comes from their extension. A picture that Word, Excel or Pages put next to copied text is not recorded as an image: the text is. Passwords are only ever kept as plain text: an image or files marked as concealed are never recorded. Settings → Storage counts rich text, images (with their thumbnails) and files separately, and the size on disk includes saved images. Saved images whose entry is gone are deleted at launch.
- History survives quitting the app: it is saved on your Mac only, in the app's sandbox container, never synced and left out of Time Machine backups. Saved passwords are encrypted with a key kept in your Keychain; without Keychain access they are kept in memory only. Settings → Storage says it is saved on this Mac only and warns when the history or a password cannot be saved. Each change is written in one transaction, so a failed write leaves the saved history as it was, and the next change writes what it missed; it never touches entries it did not load. A history that cannot be opened is tried again and, while it is a database, left where it is and kept in memory for that launch; it is set aside as `History.store.damaged` only when it is not a database or failed on 3 launches in a row. Images are kept while such a copy exists. The copy counts toward the size on disk, Settings → Storage explains it, and it is deleted a week after it was set aside, by Clear History, or when Save passwords is turned off.
- History window on a global shortcut (⌥⌘V by default, changeable in Settings), on top of any app, with the search field at its top edge, like Spotlight.
- Search, arrow keys to select, Enter to copy an entry back, Esc to close.
- Previews in the history window. Image rows show a thumbnail (loaded as the row appears, with a placeholder until then) with the format and size under it; file rows show the file's icon, its name, how many files there are and a mark when a file may have been moved or deleted, checked when the row appears. → shows the selected entry larger, ← or Esc closes it, and the preview follows ↑ and ↓; rows also offer Show Preview in their context menu and to VoiceOver. An image is read in full only for its preview and dropped when it closes; styled text is shown with its formatting but without pictures or links; files are listed with icon, name, size and the same mark. Passwords are never previewed.
- Thumbnails of copied photo and video files. When you copy photos or videos in Finder, the app reads each of the first 4 files of the copy once, right then, to make a PNG thumbnail of at most 256 pixels with QuickLook (a frame for a video): after a relaunch the sandbox no longer lets it read them. Only the thumbnail is kept, saved with the entry like an image's thumbnail and deleted with it; the file is never copied. The files are recorded at once and their thumbnails appear a moment later, so no copy made meanwhile is missed. File rows show the first file's thumbnail, with a play badge for a video, and the preview shows each file's thumbnail; VoiceOver says "Photo" or "Video" with the name. Only PNG, JPEG, HEIC, TIFF, GIF, WebP and BMP images of at most 120 megapixels whose data is the format their type names, and QuickTime, MPEG-4 and M4V movies that hold their own media, get thumbnails: SVG, PDF, reference movies and other formats keep their icons. Files that are not downloaded from iCloud, are on a network volume, cannot be read or take longer than 1.5 seconds (2 seconds for the whole copy) keep their icons. Copying the same files again fills in thumbnails they lacked and does not make the others again. Thumbnails count toward the history's size and its size on disk.
- With nothing to list (empty history or no matches) the history window shrinks to the search field and a one-line message, like Spotlight, and grows downward when items appear; the search field stays in place.
- Settings: history limit from 10 to 1000 entries with confirmation before removing old ones, clear history (also deletes it from disk, with any copy of a history that could not be opened).
- Settings → Storage: how much the history takes, split into text and passwords, its size on disk, and how much memory the app uses. A size limit (100 MB, 500 MB, 1 GB or no limit; 1 GB unless you chose another) asks before removing old items and says how much space that frees. Clear History says how many items and how much space it removes.
- Pauses while the Mac sleeps or the screen is locked. While the history window is closed and nothing was copied for a minute, the app lets macOS slow its clipboard checks down to save energy (App Nap); the first copy after a quiet spell may then be picked up a few seconds late.
- A long history is loaded in the background at launch, and Settings → Storage measures the size on disk in the background from a running total of saved images, so neither holds up the app.
- History window opts out of screen sharing and recordings (some newer capture methods may still show it), and closes when the Mac sleeps, the screen locks or another user switches in.
- Passwords are not kept by default: items marked as concealed, and anything copied while a known password manager is in front, are skipped. Items marked as transient or auto-generated are always skipped.
- A password copied without a marker leaves the history when its password manager clears the clipboard within 3 minutes of the copy and the clipboard stays empty. Only text is taken for such a password; an app that empties the clipboard just before writing to it removes nothing.
- Settings → Passwords → Save passwords (off by default): passwords are kept hidden in the history, can be revealed one at a time with an eye button, are left out of search and are removed after 1 minute, 10 minutes or 1 hour. Turning it off removes them at once. A password copied back from the history is marked as concealed, so other clipboard managers skip it.
- Passwords saved on disk are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key is created on this Mac and kept in your Keychain, never synced to iCloud, and the Keychain is asked for it, in the background, only when a password is saved or a saved one is loaded; if it cannot be read, new passwords are kept in memory only, and passwords saved before stay on disk unopened (they still expire) until the key can be read again, after you unlock the Mac or at the next launch.
- English and Russian localization.
- Requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later.
- No menu bar or Dock icon. Sandboxed without network access: the app itself makes no connections.
- Settings open on first launch and explain how to open the history, with a button to quit the app.
