# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

First version, 0.1.0. Not released yet. Requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later.

### Recording

- Clipboard history in the background, with the app each copy came from. Text is kept up to 1 MB per copy; larger text is skipped.
- Styled text is copied back with its formatting and its plain text. RTF over 1 MB is kept as its plain text; styled text whose plain text is over 1 MB is skipped without reading the RTF.
- Images (PNG, JPEG, HEIC or TIFF, up to 20 MB) are saved as files in the app's container, excluded from backups, with a thumbnail of at most 256 pixels, and are copied back in their original format. Image data that is not the format it was copied as is not recorded, and an image over 120 megapixels is kept without a thumbnail and never decoded. Copying the same image again moves its entry to the top without saving it twice. An image is listed as soon as it is copied and saved in the background, so copies made meanwhile are not missed and it can be copied back or previewed at once; quitting waits up to 2 seconds for images still being saved. Clear History and the limits never delete what an image being saved is about to use.
- Copied files (up to 100 at once) are recorded by path and name only, never their contents, and are copied back as file references; their sizes are read just after the copy, in the background. Paths on network volumes and under `/net` or `/Network` are never looked up: those files show no size or thumbnail, and their icon comes from their extension.
- Thumbnails of copied photo and video files. When you copy photos or videos in Finder, the app reads each of the first 4 files of the copy once, right then, to make a PNG thumbnail of at most 256 pixels with QuickLook (a frame for a video): after a relaunch the sandbox no longer lets it read them. Only the thumbnail is kept, saved with the entry like an image's thumbnail and deleted with it; the file is never copied. The files are recorded at once and their thumbnails appear a moment later, also in a row or preview already on screen, so no copy made meanwhile is missed. File rows show the first file's thumbnail, with a play badge for a video, and the preview shows each file's thumbnail; VoiceOver says "Photo" or "Video" with the name. Only PNG, JPEG, HEIC, TIFF, GIF, WebP and BMP images of at most 120 megapixels whose data is the format their type names, and QuickTime, MPEG-4 and M4V movies that hold their own media, get thumbnails: SVG, PDF, reference movies and other formats keep their icons. Files that are not downloaded from iCloud, are on a network volume, cannot be read or take longer than 1.5 seconds (2 seconds for the whole copy) keep their icons. Copying the same files again fills in thumbnails they lacked and does not make the others again. Thumbnails count toward the history's size and its size on disk.
- A picture that Word, Excel or Pages put next to copied text is not recorded as an image: the text is.
- Pauses while the Mac sleeps or the screen is locked. While the history window is closed and nothing was copied for a minute, the app lets macOS slow its clipboard checks down to save energy (App Nap); the first copy after a quiet spell may then be picked up a few seconds late.

### History window

- History window on a global shortcut (⌥⌘V by default, changeable in Settings), on top of any app, with the search field at its top edge, like Spotlight.
- Search, arrow keys to select, Enter to copy an entry back, Esc to close. Search looks at the first 10,000 characters of each entry.
- With nothing to list (empty history or no matches) the history window shrinks to the search field and a one-line message, like Spotlight, and grows downward when items appear; the search field stays in place.
- Previews in the history window. Image rows show a thumbnail (loaded as the row appears, with a placeholder until then) with the format and size under it; file rows show the file's icon, its name, how many files there are and a mark when a file may have been moved or deleted, checked when the row appears. → shows the selected entry larger, ← or Esc closes it, and the preview follows ↑ and ↓; rows also offer Show Preview in their context menu and to VoiceOver. An image is read in full only for its preview and dropped when it closes; styled text is shown with its formatting but without pictures or links; files are listed with icon, name, size and the same mark. Passwords are never previewed.
- Delete one entry from the history window with ⌘⌫, Delete in a row's context menu or the VoiceOver action, without a confirmation. The entry on the clipboard now can be deleted too: when the clipboard still holds exactly what the app recorded for it (nothing was copied since), it is emptied, and the app does not record the empty clipboard or take it for a password manager clearing a password; anything else on the clipboard is left alone. The window stays open and selects the next entry, or the previous one after the last. The entry is deleted from disk at once; ⌘Z brings it back, with its image or thumbnails, while the window is open, and its files are deleted when the window closes or another entry is deleted. An image still being saved cannot be brought back. Plain Delete still edits the search text, and ⌘Z with nothing to bring back still undoes typing.
- Pin entries: ⌘P, Pin in a row's context menu or the VoiceOver action pins the selected entry, and ⌘P or Unpin unpins it. Pinned entries are listed in their own Pinned section, below the entry on the clipboard and above History, in the order they were pinned and with a pin mark instead of the time; a new copy goes on the clipboard row and never moves above them, and search and the arrow keys cover every section. Copying a pinned entry back keeps it in its place. An unpinned entry goes to the top of History. Passwords cannot be pinned (Pin is dimmed): they keep their own expiry. ⌘⌫ deletes a pinned entry like any other, and ⌘Z brings it back pinned. ⌘P never prints. Pins are saved with the history and survive a relaunch.
- Control Center button on macOS 26 and later: Clipboard History opens the history window, and leaves it open if it already is (the shortcut toggles it). The app performs the button's action: it is started if it is not running, without a Dock icon and without being brought to the front. Pressed while the history is still loading at launch, the window opens once it has loaded. The action is not offered in Shortcuts or Spotlight.
- History window opts out of screen sharing and recordings (some newer capture methods may still show it), and closes when the Mac sleeps, the screen locks or another user switches in.

### Saved history and limits

- History survives quitting the app: it is saved on your Mac only, in the app's sandbox container, never synced and left out of Time Machine backups. Saved passwords are encrypted with a key kept in your Keychain; without Keychain access they are kept in memory only. Settings → Storage says it is saved on this Mac only and warns when the history or a password cannot be saved. Each change is written in one transaction, so a failed write leaves the saved history as it was, and the next change writes what it missed; it never touches entries it did not load. A history that cannot be opened is tried again and, while it is a database, left where it is and kept in memory for that launch; it is set aside as `History.store.damaged` only when it is not a database or failed on 3 launches in a row. Images are kept while such a copy exists. The copy counts toward the size on disk, Settings → Storage explains it, and it is deleted a week after it was set aside, by Clear History, or when Save passwords is turned off.
- Settings → History: a limit from 10 to 1000 entries (50 by default) that asks before removing old ones.
- Pinned entries are never removed by the entry, size or age limits and do not count toward the entry and size limits; they count toward the history size in Settings → Storage. When the history has pinned entries, the question before a lower limit says they are kept, and the size limit says it does not count them.
- Clear History in Settings asks first and says how many items and how much space it removes. It deletes the history from disk too, with any copy of a history that could not be opened. Pinned entries are kept unless "Also remove pinned entries" is checked in the question; when they are kept, a history that could not be opened stays on disk too, since it may hold pinned entries.
- History older than 8 hours is removed by default. Settings → History → Remove items after: 1 hour, 8 hours, 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or Off; a shorter limit asks before removing items and says how much space that frees. Pinned entries are kept. Items are removed while the app runs, right after the Mac wakes or the screen is unlocked, and at launch; an open history window drops them as they go. Passwords still go after their own, shorter time.
- Settings → Storage: how much the history takes, split into text, rich text, images (with their thumbnails), files and passwords, its size on disk (saved images included), and how much memory the app uses. The passwords' size is shown rounded up to whole kilobytes, also in the history size and in what Clear History or a lower limit frees, so it never tells how long a password is. A size limit (100 MB, 500 MB, 1 GB or no limit; 1 GB unless you chose another) asks before removing old items and says how much space that frees.
- Saved images and thumbnails that no entry uses any more are deleted at launch.
- A long history is loaded in the background at launch, and Settings → Storage measures the size on disk in the background from a running total of saved images, so neither holds up the app.

### Passwords

- Passwords are not kept by default: items marked as concealed, and anything copied while a known password manager is in front, are skipped. Items marked as transient or auto-generated are always skipped.
- A password copied without a marker leaves the history when its password manager clears the clipboard within 3 minutes of the copy and the clipboard stays empty. Only text is taken for such a password; an app that empties the clipboard just before writing to it removes nothing. A clipboard cleared while the Mac sleeps or is locked is noticed after waking or unlocking; it counts when the Mac went to sleep or was locked within 3 minutes of the copy and nothing else was copied meanwhile.
- Settings → Passwords → Save passwords (off by default): passwords are kept hidden in the history, can be revealed one at a time with an eye button, are left out of search and are removed after 1 minute, 10 minutes or 1 hour; a password whose time is up while the Mac sleeps or is locked goes as soon as the Mac or its displays wake or the screen is unlocked. Turning it off removes them at once. A password copied back from the history is marked as concealed, so other clipboard managers skip it. When a saved password expires or is removed because the setting was turned off, the clipboard is emptied too if it still holds exactly that password, also one copied back from the history; anything copied since is left alone.
- Passwords saved on disk are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key is created on this Mac and kept in your Keychain, never synced to iCloud, and the Keychain is asked for it, in the background, only when a password is saved or a saved one is loaded; if it cannot be read, new passwords are kept in memory only, and passwords saved before stay on disk unopened (they still expire) until the key can be read again, after you unlock the Mac or at the next launch.
- Passwords are only ever kept as plain text: an image or files marked as concealed are never recorded.

### App

- No menu bar or Dock icon. Sandboxed without network access: the app itself makes no connections.
- Settings open on first launch and explain how to open the history, with a button to quit the app.
- English and Russian localization.

### Development builds

- Saved history from development builds is reset. The saved format now has one layout for every kind of entry and no versions; the first launch of this build deletes the history that earlier development builds saved (the database with its side files, a damaged copy and the saved images) without reading it, and starts empty. The history now lives in `Application Support/ShowMyCopy/History`.
